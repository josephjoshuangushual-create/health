High blood pressure — hypertension — affects nearly half of adults, yet most people can't feel it. That's exactly what makes it dangerous: it quietly strains your arteries, heart, and kidneys for years. The upside is that few chronic conditions respond so well to changes you control.

## What the numbers mean

Blood pressure is written as two numbers, for example 120/80 mmHg:

| Category | Systolic (top) | Diastolic (bottom) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Below 120 | and below 80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and below 80 |
| Stage 1 | 130–139 | or 80–89 |
| Stage 2 | 140 or higher | or 90 or higher |

A single high reading isn't a diagnosis. Clinicians confirm hypertension with repeated measurements, often including readings taken at home.

## Why it matters

Untreated hypertension raises the risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure, kidney disease, and vision loss. Because damage accumulates silently, early detection and steady control matter more than any single reading.

## Proven ways to lower it

- **Reduce sodium.** Aim for less than 2,300 mg per day; most benefit comes from cutting processed and restaurant foods.
- **Follow the DASH pattern.** Vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and low-fat dairy can lower systolic pressure by several points.
- **Move regularly.** 150 minutes a week of moderate activity meaningfully lowers pressure.
- **Limit alcohol and quit smoking.**
- **Manage stress and sleep.** Poor sleep, including untreated sleep apnea, drives blood pressure up.

## When medication is needed

Lifestyle changes are the foundation, but many people also need medication — and that's not a failure. Several well-studied drug classes (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, and thiazide diuretics) are safe and effective. The right choice depends on your other conditions.

## The bottom line

Check your numbers, know your category, and treat hypertension as the long game it is. Small, consistent changes — plus medication when appropriate — dramatically cut your risk of the events that make high blood pressure dangerous.
