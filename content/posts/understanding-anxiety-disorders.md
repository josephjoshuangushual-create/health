Everyone feels anxious sometimes. But when worry becomes persistent, intense, and interferes with daily life, it may be an anxiety disorder — the most common category of mental health condition worldwide.

> **When to seek help now:** If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line immediately. In the U.S., you can call or text **988** for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

## What counts as an anxiety disorder?

Clinicians distinguish everyday stress from a disorder based on duration, intensity, and impairment. Common types include:

- **Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD)** — chronic, excessive worry about many areas of life.
- **Panic disorder** — recurrent, unexpected panic attacks with physical symptoms like chest tightness and shortness of breath.
- **Social anxiety disorder** — intense fear of judgment in social situations.
- **Specific phobias** — disproportionate fear of a particular object or situation.

## Recognizing the symptoms

Anxiety shows up in the body as much as the mind:

- Racing heart, muscle tension, or restlessness
- Difficulty concentrating or a "blank" mind
- Sleep disruption
- Avoidance of feared situations
- A persistent sense of dread

## Why it happens

Anxiety disorders arise from a mix of genetics, brain chemistry, temperament, and life experience. No single cause explains every case — which is also why treatment is individualized.

## Treatments that work

### Psychotherapy

**Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)** has the strongest evidence. It helps you identify and reframe anxious thought patterns and gradually face avoided situations.

### Medication

SSRIs and SNRIs are common first-line medications and can be highly effective, often alongside therapy. A clinician can help weigh benefits and side effects.

### Lifestyle foundations

Regular exercise, consistent sleep, limiting caffeine and alcohol, and mindfulness practices all measurably reduce anxiety for many people — best used to support, not replace, evidence-based treatment.

## The takeaway

Anxiety disorders are common, real, and highly treatable. If anxiety is interfering with your work, relationships, or wellbeing, a primary care clinician or mental health professional is the right place to start.
