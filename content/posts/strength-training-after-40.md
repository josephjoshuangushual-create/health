Somewhere around your mid-thirties, you begin losing muscle mass — a process called sarcopenia that accelerates with each decade if left unchecked. The single most effective countermeasure isn't a supplement or a gadget. It's resistance training.

## Why muscle matters more as you age

Muscle does far more than move you around. Greater muscle mass and strength are associated with:

- Better blood-sugar control and insulin sensitivity
- Stronger bones and lower fracture risk
- Fewer falls and greater independence later in life
- Higher resting metabolism

Grip strength — a simple proxy for overall strength — is one of the better predictors of long-term health outcomes researchers have found.

## You don't need to train like an athlete

The evidence is encouraging for beginners: two to three sessions per week is enough to build meaningful strength. A simple, effective program covers the major movement patterns:

1. **Squat** (or leg press / sit-to-stand)
2. **Hinge** (deadlift, hip thrust)
3. **Push** (push-up, overhead or chest press)
4. **Pull** (row, lat pulldown)
5. **Carry / core** (loaded carries, planks)

## Progress the smart way

- Start with a weight you can control for 8–12 reps.
- Add a little resistance or a rep when the last set feels manageable — this is *progressive overload*.
- Prioritize form over load, especially in the first months.
- Rest 48 hours before training the same muscles again.

## Recovery is part of the program

Muscle grows during recovery, not during the workout. Protein intake around 1.2–1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight, adequate sleep, and rest days all support progress.

## Getting started safely

If you have heart disease, joint problems, or have been inactive for a long time, check with your clinician before starting. Consider a few sessions with a qualified trainer to learn the movements — good technique early pays off for decades.

## The takeaway

It's never too late to start. Strength gained in your forties, fifties, and beyond translates directly into a more capable, independent, and resilient future self.
