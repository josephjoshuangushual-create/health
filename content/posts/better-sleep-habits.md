Sleep is not a luxury — it's a pillar of health on par with diet and exercise. Chronic poor sleep is linked to heart disease, weight gain, impaired immunity, and mood disorders. The encouraging part: most people can improve their sleep with a few consistent habits.

## Start with a consistent schedule

Your body runs on an internal clock. Going to bed and waking up at roughly the same time — even on weekends — is one of the most powerful and underrated interventions.

## Build a wind-down routine

- Dim the lights an hour before bed.
- Put screens away, or at least silence notifications; bright light and mental stimulation delay sleep onset.
- Do something calming and repeatable: reading, stretching, or a warm shower.

## Optimize the bedroom

- **Cool:** most people sleep best around 18°C (65°F).
- **Dark:** blackout curtains or an eye mask help.
- **Quiet:** earplugs or white noise can mask disruptions.

## Watch the inputs

- **Caffeine** has a long half-life — avoid it after early afternoon.
- **Alcohol** may make you drowsy but fragments sleep later in the night.
- **Large late meals** can cause reflux and restlessness.

## What to do when you can't sleep

If you're awake for more than about 20 minutes, get up and do something quiet and dimly lit until you feel sleepy. Lying in bed frustrated trains your brain to associate bed with wakefulness.

## Myths worth dropping

- **"Everyone needs exactly 8 hours."** Needs vary; most adults do well on 7–9.
- **"A nightcap helps you sleep."** It worsens sleep quality.
- **"You can fully catch up on weekends."** Weekend recovery helps a little but doesn't erase a week of deprivation.

## When to see a professional

Loud snoring with gasping, persistent insomnia, or daytime sleepiness despite adequate time in bed can signal a treatable disorder like sleep apnea. If good habits aren't enough, talk to a clinician — effective treatments exist.

## The bottom line

Better sleep rarely comes from a single product. It comes from consistent timing, a calm wind-down, a good sleep environment, and smart daytime habits. Start with one change tonight.
